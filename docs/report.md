# Budgeted Semantic–Hypergraph Agglomeration over an Evolving Knowledge Hypergraph

**Alexey Reznik**

**Abstract.** We build a budgeted, laminar, three-level abstraction of a temporal knowledge hypergraph cut into four
cumulative snapshots at year cutoffs. The method (SHAC) is a deterministic greedy agglomeration whose merge score mixes, on a common
rank scale, a name-similarity term and a structural term computed on the collapsed hyper-edge incidence, with a
localised prior from the previous snapshot. Laminarity, exact budgets and the hyper-edge collapse rule hold by
construction. Coherence is graded with a signal the method never sees, bibliographic coupling of concept-disjoint
papers, against a configuration null and random labels; stability against perturbation and against a random-attachment
null; label faithfulness against held-out evidence; and extrinsic utility by drill-down retrieval against flat search.
Each verdict is computed from the run outputs and summarised in Table 13 (§5.4).

*Every table is generated by `make_tables.py` from `out/` (`metrics.json`, `run_info.json`, `run_config.toml`,
`<t>/coarse_hyperedges.json`; `make tables`), including the verdicts of Table 13; figures are generated by
`eval/figures.py`. The text quotes no result values or verdicts; it interprets the tables. Snapshots are written ≤t; levels are L0 (coarsest), L1 and L2 (finest).*

## 1. Problem definition

The Temporal Knowledge Hypergraph (TKH) represents scientific literature as typed nodes (methods, tasks, datasets,
metrics, …) joined by dated hyper-edges of arbitrary arity. The assignment asks for a multi-resolution semantic
abstraction: per snapshot H(t), a laminar sequence of partitions under hard per-level budgets (P1, P2) whose
super-nodes are close in meaning (P3), computed on the hypergraph rather than a pairwise projection (P4), evolving
smoothly with trackable identities (P5), and labelled without unsupported claims (P6). The tasks cover the method and
its formal statement (T2), temporal coupling (T3), an implemented hyper-edge collapse rule (T4), labelling (T5) and a
circularity-safe evaluation (T6). We use the provided export, cut into cumulative snapshots at the cutoffs of
Table 2 (sizes in Table 1); the run's parameters are listed in Table 2.

## 2. Formal statement

**Input.** V_t is the set of concept nodes of snapshot t (method, task, problem, technique, component, dataset,
metric), n_t = |V_t|; an edge is in t if its paper is from year ≤ t. The clustering hypergraph G_t = (V_t, E_t) holds
the edges of six method-centred relations (addresses, solves, extends, uses_component, uses_technique, evaluated_on)
restricted to V_t, with *hubs* (concepts in more than a fraction h of the snapshot's papers) removed and edges with
fewer than two members dropped. Citations, claims, authorship and titles never enter G_t. Weights are
w_e = 1/(|e| − 1), rescaled to equal total weight per relation. Each node has a text vector z_v: character 3–5-gram
TF-IDF of its name and aliases, reduced by SVD, centred and L2-normalised.

**Output.** For budgets (N_0, N_1, N_2), partitions P_{t,0} ⪰ P_{t,1} ⪰ P_{t,2} of V_t with |P_{t,k}| = min(N_k, n_t),
each refining the one above. Non-concept nodes (articles, claims, authors, cited works) are then attached to the
super-node of one concept anchor.

**Criterion.** A nested partition under hard budgets contains NP-hard single-level cases (modularity, normalised cut),
so SHAC (semantic–hypergraph agglomerative coarsening) optimises no global objective. It is a deterministic greedy
procedure: level k starts from the super-nodes of level k+1 (the singletons of V_t at the finest level) and merges the
feasible pair with the largest score until min(N_k, #items) remain:

$$ s(A,B) = \alpha\,F_S\big(S(A,B)\big) + (1-\alpha)\,F_H\big(H(A,B)\big), $$
$$ s_t(A,B) = (1-\gamma)\,s(A,B) + \gamma\,\Pi(A,B)\ \text{ if both } A, B \text{ hold stable old nodes, else } s(A,B). $$

- **Semantic term** S(A,B) = ⟨z̄_A, z̄_B⟩, the inner product of mean member vectors, i.e. average linkage.
- **Structural term** H(A,B) = Σ_{e: A∈e, B∈e} w_e / √(d_A d_B), with hyperdegree d_A = Σ_{e∋A} w_e and A ∈ e iff e has
  an endpoint in A, i.e. computed on the *collapsed* incidence of §4. The numerator is the merge's reduction of the
  connectivity cut (Proposition 1, §4); the normaliser stops high-degree super-nodes from absorbing everything.
- **Rank calibration.** F_S is the mid-rank empirical CDF of S over all item pairs at the start of the level; F_H that
  of H over pairs with H > 0, with F_H(0) = 0; both frozen per level. Ranks map incommensurable scales to [0, 1], so α
  is the explicit structure–semantics weight (α = 0 structure only, α = 1 semantics only).
- **Temporal prior** Π(A,B) = Σ_c M_{A,c} M_{B,c} / (u_A u_B), the share of pairs of stable members of A and B that
  shared a level-k super-node at t−1 (M_{A,c}: A's stable members in c; u_A = |A ∩ U_t|, §3).
- **Feasibility** |A| + |B| ≤ ρ·n_t/N_k (caps in Table 1). Ties: S and H are rounded to 1e-12 and scores to 1e-9;
  ties of s_t are broken by s, then by the lowest item index.

γ was fixed before any result, and no current result selects α or ρ (values in Table 2).

**Lemma (budget feasibility).** If ρ ≥ 2 and K > N_k items remain, the two smallest have total size
≤ 2n_t/K < ρ·n_t/N_k, so a feasible merge exists and every level meets its budget exactly.

**Complexity.** Per level with n items, m edges and LSA dimension d: O(n²(m + d)) initialisation, O(n(m + d)) per
merge (O(n²) worst case), O(n²) memory; the build runtime is in Table 2.

**Rejected alternatives.** Hypergraph modularity (h-Louvain [1]) sets the community count only through a resolution
parameter and does not nest across resolutions, whereas P2 is a hard constraint and P1 requires nesting. A two-stage
scheme (structure, then semantics) would freeze single-paper bundles before names could join them across papers.

## 3. Method and literature positioning

**Reconciling the signals.** We assume that names relate generic tasks and techniques better than proper-named
methods (a "-Net" suffix is not a family), and that structure relates mainly concepts co-used within one paper, since
every clustering edge comes from one paper's relations around its method. The rank mix lets either signal decide each merge: a pair sharing
no edge gets no structural credit and can win only on semantics, at weight α. Because the trade-off is one scalar on a
common scale, it is evaluable: the coherence ruler of §5 sees neither signal, so α = 0, intermediate values and α = 1
can be compared on equal footing. The configuration sweep (`make sweep`) predates the final verification fixes and is
not used, so the chosen α is a stated prior, not a measured optimum.

**Temporal stability (T3).** The stable set U_t holds old concept nodes that no new paper's clustering edge touches and
that are unaffected by hub changes (sizes in Table 1). Only pairs that both hold stable nodes receive Π; new and
touched nodes are placed by structure and names alone. Change can thus occur where the corpus changed, while untouched
regions are pulled, not forced, to stay together. Identities and the birth/growth/merge/split log come from
largest-overlap matching (`tkh/temporal.py`), checked by `validate.py`; deaths cannot occur because snapshots only
grow. The cost is O(n²N_k) extra initialisation and path dependence.

The hypergraph setting needs this because a clique projection counts a multi-way edge once per member pair, rewarding
already-large paper bundles (§4); the temporal setting needs it because static or ratio-controlled coarseners give
neither persistent identities nor fixed display sizes. The structural null follows the hypergraph configuration model
[5], and Π is an evolutionary-clustering term [6]. The contribution is their integration under hard budgets with an
evaluation that does not reuse the method's signals.

## 4. Coarsening decision

For a fine edge e of reduced arity n_e with endpoint counts c_S(e), let λ_e = #{S : c_S(e) > 0} and m_e = max_S c_S(e).
`tkh/coarsen.py` assigns every edge to exactly one of three classes:

- **m_e = n_e (λ_e = 1): internal.** It drops out of every H numerator, but its weight stays in d_A, damping
  super-nodes that have already absorbed much of their incident weight.
- **1 < m_e < n_e (2 ≤ λ_e < n_e): partial.** One coarse hyper-edge over its λ_e super-nodes; counts kept as metadata;
  presence scored.
- **m_e = 1 (λ_e = n_e): uncollapsed.** The fine edge itself, one endpoint per super-node; scored as partial.

Separately, an edge touching three or more super-nodes (λ_e ≥ 3) is flagged **multi-way**. The flag is not a class: a
multi-way edge is partial or uncollapsed. It stays one hyper-edge, is never split into pairs, and adds w_e to the H
numerator of every pair of super-nodes it touches.

**Proposition 1.** Let C = Σ_e w_e(λ_e − 1). Merging A and B reduces C by exactly Σ_{e∋A,B} w_e, since each such edge
loses one distinct super-node and no other edge changes. The structural numerator is thus the gain in the standard
(λ − 1) connectivity objective of hypergraph partitioning, for any arity.

**Why this rule.** Every clustering edge is a star around one paper's method. A count-based (clique) score
c_A(e)·c_B(e) pays a super-node more the more of that star it already holds, reinforcing paper bundling; presence
counts each edge once per super-node. The rule is the one the method applies: the incidence is updated after every
merge (`_Level.merge`), and the same partition writes `out/<t>/coarse_hyperedges.json` (Table 5). Native and clique
scores diverge whenever a shared edge has two or more endpoints on one side; Table 5 counts these merges, but whether
a clique build would have *chosen* other merges was not measured. **Losses:** endpoint multiplicity in the score,
which fine members took part, coarse-edge identity across levels, and merging of parallel coarse edges
(score-neutral, as H and d_A are sums over edges).

## 5. Evaluation

**Coherence and the circularity hazard.** Clustering uses name n-grams and six method-centred relations, so a
coherence score built on either would grade the method with its own ruler. `eval/coherence.py` neutralises this in four
steps. (i) *Held-out signal:* bibliographic coupling c_pq, the IDF-weighted Salton cosine of two papers' cited-work
sets; citations never enter G_t, z_v or Π. (ii) *Residual name filter:* citations to works named like one of the
paper's own concepts are dropped, so "extends X / cites X" cannot leak; an exact and a stricter fuzzy filter are both
run (Table 6). (iii) *No-overlap pairs:* only paper pairs sharing no concept node or name are scored. (iv) *Period
control:* same-era papers cite the same works, so the lift Σ a_pq c_pq / Σ a_pq e_pq standardises by e_pq, the mean
coupling of no-overlap pairs in the same period stratum (a_pq: co-assignment of the papers' concepts; 1 = chance). The
lift is compared with a **configuration null** (full rebuilds on a type- and period-preserving hypergraph
configuration model [5]; it keeps the real names, so it asks what the hyper-edge structure adds beyond degrees) and
**random labels** (permutations within type × entry interval over the fixed tree, which keep every super-node's size).
A cell is *robust* only if it beats both nulls under every period scheme of Table 2 at its significance level; the
exact filter is primary, and
p-values are not corrected across cells. A snapshot whose primary (interval) scheme has a single period stratum is
period-controlled neither by that scheme nor by the random-label null, and is marked not evaluable.

**Stability.** As the brief asks, a fraction of all hyper-edges is removed for several seeds (stratifying by period ×
relation is our addition) and the chain rebuilt; snapshots are scored against the unperturbed run by per-level ARI and
HARI, a level-pooled laminar ARI Σ_k(I_k − E_k)/Σ_k(M_k − E_k) dominated by L0 (t-intervals). Cross-snapshot agreement
on common concept nodes (intervals over half-subsamples) is tested against a **new-paper null** that keeps the real
past graph and hierarchy and redraws which old nodes new papers attach to. *Localisation* compares reassignment
(1 − Jaccard of co-members) of old nodes touched by new papers with untouched ones.

**Label faithfulness.** L0–L1 labels and one-sentence glosses come from the labeller (Table 2), which sees one half of
a seeded, kind-stratified 50/50 split of the group's snapshot evidence (concept names with types and aliases,
clustering facts, claims, future-work topics, paper titles; no citations; both halves in `out/<t>/label_inputs.json`),
plus, as contrast, a few seen-half concept names of the same-level groups sharing the most clustering edges with it. A
judge of another family splits label + gloss into at most three atomic claims and checks each against the
**held-out** half, citing an existing line; the over-claim rate is the share of labels with an unsupported claim.
Restating the labeller's input cannot pass, but both halves use the same kinds of text, so shared vocabulary is not
controlled. Labels whose decomposition fails its content check are judged as [label, gloss]. Deterministic checks
flag post-cutoff years, named non-members and future entities.

**Extrinsic utility.** An agent answers the questions of `data/questions.csv` at the last snapshot (type A, specific
search; type B, synthesis), descending from L0 with a beam scored by keyword overlap with label, gloss and member text,
against flat search with the same scorer. Only the evaluator reads the ground truth; one step is one inspected node.

## 6. Future work

**Assumed, not verified:** within-paper relations indicate relatedness; names carry weak semantics and relate generic
concepts better than proper-named methods (§3); coupling between
concept-disjoint papers proxies closeness in meaning; export-wide names carry no post-cutoff information; entry follows
publication years; untouched structure should keep its grouping (tested against the null, Table 9). **Simplifications:**
irrevocable greedy merges, dense O(n²) memory, a lexical retrieval scorer, one labeller, one judge, one evidence split.
**Verified by construction or by computed tests:** P1–P2 at every snapshot (`validate.py`); the applied T4 rule
(Table 5); the verdicts of Table 13 (uncorrected tests, this corpus only).

1. **Controls:** γ = 0 runs and a fresh α/ρ/hub sweep selected on held-out halves of the citation ruler.
2. **Multiplicity-aware collapse:** H_β = Σ_e w_e (c_{A,e} c_{B,e})^β/√(d_A d_B), from β = 0 (shipped) to β = 1
   (clique), to quantify what the projection loses.
3. **Graded structure:** multi-hop affinity (spectral smoothing as in [4]), since H is zero for every pair that shares
   no clustering edge.
4. **Levels below both nulls (Table 13):** for any level listed there, per-super-node coupled-pair diagnostics, and a
   rebuild of that level from task and dataset families or a stronger independent embedding.
5. **Labels:** judge validation (known answers, second judge, human ratings), null-tree baselines, repeated splits.
6. **Retrieval:** paired bootstrap CIs, a random-tree null, held-out tuning, earlier snapshots.
7. **Scale:** sparse k-NN candidates and DynHAC-style dirty-region updates [3].
