# Multi-resolution semantic abstraction over an evolving knowledge hypergraph

**Method: SHAC-T**, semantic–hypergraph agglomerative coarsening with temporal coupling.
Data: the collection-10 TKH export (52 papers, cumulative snapshots ≤2020 / ≤2022 / ≤2024 /
≤2026, budgets 15 / 60 / 240). Every number table below is generated by `make_tables.py`
from `out/metrics.json`. The full formal statement is in
[docs/formal_statement.md](docs/formal_statement.md), the method diagnostics in
[notebooks/03_method.ipynb](notebooks/03_method.ipynb).

## 1. Problem and formal statement (Deliverable 0)

At each cutoff *t*, the concept nodes V_t (method, task, problem, technique, component,
dataset, metric) are partitioned into P_{t,2} ⊑ P_{t,1} ⊑ P_{t,0}, with exactly
min(N_k, |V_t|) super-nodes at level k. All other nodes (articles, authors, claims, cited
works, future topics) are anchored to the super-node of one concept node of their paper.
Super-nodes carry persistent ids and an event log across snapshots.

**Criterion.** SHAC-T does not claim to optimise a global objective. It is a
deterministic greedy procedure with a stepwise criterion. Level by level, from finest to
coarsest, it merges the feasible pair (A, B) that maximises

  s_t(A,B) = (1−γ)·[α·F_S(S(A,B)) + (1−α)·F_H(H(A,B))] + γ·Π(A,B).

- **H, the structural term.** The reduction of the hypergraph's weighted connectivity cut
  Σ_e w_e(λ_e − 1) that the merge causes (Proposition 1 of the formal statement), divided
  by √(d_A d_B). It is computed on the *current collapsed incidence*, so the T4 rule is
  applied at every merge.
- **S, the semantic term.** The mean pairwise cosine of centred character-n-gram LSA
  vectors of the node names, fitted per snapshot.
- **F_S and F_H.** Their mid-rank empirical CDFs, so α weighs two percentile scales and
  not two incomparable magnitudes.
- **Π, the temporal term.** The share of old-member pairs co-clustered at the same level
  at t−1 (evolutionary clustering).
- **Feasibility.** |A| + |B| ≤ ρ·|V_t|/N_k.

**Structure–semantics trade-off.** S and H disagree on almost every pair: H is zero for
more than 99% of pairs, because the hyper-edges are per-paper stars. Rank-normalising both
and mixing with one weight α makes the trade-off a single, interpretable parameter that
can be swept, instead of a byproduct of scales. Its cost is that magnitudes within a
signal are lost.

**Guaranteed by construction:**
- P1, laminarity (level k merges level-k+1 super-nodes);
- P2, the budgets (a lemma: with ρ ≥ 2 a feasible pair always exists, so every level
  reaches exactly N_k);
- a bound on the largest super-node;
- hypergraph-native scoring at every level (P4);
- determinism;
- no information after the cutoff (P6 for membership).

**Measured, not guaranteed:** P3 coherence, P4 fidelity loss vs a projection, P5
stability, and P6 label faithfulness.

**Complexity.** O(n²(m + d)) per level to initialise and O(n(m + d)) per merge, with
O(n²) memory. The full four-snapshot build takes about 25 s for n ≈ 4k concept nodes.

**Rejected alternative.** Hypergraph modularity / h-Louvain (Kamiński et al. 2019;
Chodrow et al. 2021):
- it gives no control over the number of communities, which P2 makes a hard constraint;
- it has a resolution limit;
- on per-paper stars it rewards exactly the paper bundles the structural signal already
  produces.

An attributed hypergraph SBM was considered and set aside: with 554 clustering edges for
about 4k nodes, inference is under-determined, and it is not natively hierarchical with
fixed budgets.

## 2. Literature positioning

- **What it borrows from hypergraph partitioning.** The λ−1 (connectivity) metric
  comes from multilevel partitioning (hMETIS and KaHyPar). The splitting-function view
  of hypergraph cuts comes from Veldt, Benson and Kleinberg (2022). SHAC-T uses
  connectivity-cut reduction as an agglomeration gain, not as a k-way partitioning
  objective.
- **What it borrows from graph coarsening.** The idea of building a multi-resolution
  hierarchy by successive contraction comes from coarsening (Loukas 2019; HyperEF,
  Aghdaei & Feng 2022). SHAC-T contracts by an interpretable score under a size budget,
  not by spectral preservation, because the display constraint (P2) is hard.
- **What it borrows from evolutionary clustering.** The temporal term follows Chakrabarti
  et al. (KDD 2006) and the preserving-cluster-membership form of Chi et al. (KDD 2007),
  applied as a merge prior. Identities and events come from overlap matching (as in
  Greene et al. 2010).
- **What the setting changes.** Clustering edges are per-paper stars of up to 51 members, so a
  pairwise projection gives most pairs the same weight, and the method needs a score
  that counts an edge once per pair of super-nodes. The ablation below measures what the
  projection changes.

## 3. Hyper-edge collapse (T4), as implemented

An arity-n edge with endpoints in λ distinct super-nodes:
- **λ = 1 (m = n):** the edge becomes *internal*. It is kept as metadata and gives no
  affinity.
- **1 < λ < n:** it becomes a coarse edge over the λ super-nodes, keeping its endpoint
  counts.
- **λ ≥ 3:** it stays **one multi-way coarse hyper-edge** and is never split into pairs.

The merge score uses presence (λ), so multiplicity is lost from the score (kept as
metadata), as is which fine members took part. Coarse edges are written per snapshot to
`out/<t>/coarse_hyperedges.json`. Notebook 03 shows that a substantial share of coarse
edges stays multi-way at every level.

## 4. Evaluation design and circularity

All metrics use concept nodes only, against the real graph, for the model, every swept
configuration and ablation, and every null. The rules were fixed in `configs/eval.toml`
before the sweep was run.

**Coherence (E1a, primary): residual bibliographic coupling.**
- *Why it is independent.* Citations never enter the clustering. A test checks that the
  build is unchanged when `cites` edges are deleted or cited works renamed.
- *Residual filter.* Cited works whose name matches a concept of the citing paper are
  removed (an `extends X` paper cites X).
- *Score.* Coupling between papers (IDF-weighted Salton cosine) is aggregated at
  paper-pair level, weighted by how often the two papers' single-paper concepts share a
  super-node. Lift = weighted over unweighted mean coupling.
- *Strata.* Pairs are stratified by whether they share a non-hub concept, and a
  standardised pooled ratio is reported.
- *Selection vs reporting.* The cited works are split into halves: **half A is used only
  for selecting α, half B only for reporting.**

**E1b.** Ground-truth co-membership, descriptive only (12 usable questions).

**Nulls**, each run through the full pipeline:
- *struct-null:* a Curveball shuffle of the clustering edges that preserves per-period
  degree and arity;
- *sem-null:* the name texts permuted within type × period;
- *full-null:* both;
- *random-labels:* the model's tree with concept nodes permuted within type × period ×
  paper count.

**Stability (E2).**
- *Perturbation:* remove 10% of the clustering-relation edges, 5 seeds, ARI with
  t-intervals.
- *Cross-snapshot:* ARI on common nodes.
- *Change localisation:* reassignment of old nodes touched by new edges ÷ untouched.
  This is a P5 metric that a temporal prior cannot inflate.
- *References:* γ = 0 and freeze.

**Labels (E3).**
- *Circularity control.* The labeller (qwen2.5:7b) sees only a seeded half of each
  super-node's members. The judge sees the held-out half, plus claim sentences and titles
  of those members' papers (all with year ≤ cutoff).
- *Absolute rating (pre-registered).* Accurate / vague / wrong, with a swapped-gloss
  negative control.
- *Forced choice (added after the absolute rating failed its control, §5).* The judge
  must pick the true label among four same-level labels.
- *Judges.* The pre-registered judge llama3.1:8b is primary. Robustness judges come from
  two more families.

**Extrinsic task (E4).**
- *Task.* For each ground-truth question, find its expected methods.
- *Compared methods.* A best-first drill-down from the 15 roots, a flat lexical ranking,
  and personalised PageRank on the same clustering hypergraph (a strong structure-aware
  baseline), all charged one unit per item read.

**Statistics.** Four pre-registered primary tests, one per family, Holm-corrected;
everything else is descriptive.
